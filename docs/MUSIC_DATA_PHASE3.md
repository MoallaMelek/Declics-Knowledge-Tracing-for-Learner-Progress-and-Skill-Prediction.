# Real musical histories demand verified evidence

**Real musical knowledge-tracing datasets exist, but this investigation did not establish a ready, legally verified longitudinal KT table that DÉCLIC can immediately train on and redistribute.** SingPAD/SingKT offers the strongest documented combination of learner histories, completion timestamps and note outcomes; OcarinaKT offers substantial wind-instrument histories with unresolved chronology and target semantics. Neither inspected repository supplied an applicable explicit raw-data grant. Rach3 provides an explicitly licensed piano-practice route, but its four pianists and missing validated correctness labels support a bounded chronological case study rather than population-level musical mastery claims. PianoVAM supplies genuine amateur practice recordings, although repeated performer-piece coverage still needs an independent metadata count. These findings justify source-specific acquisition gates and a transparent case-study route, not replacing real musical validation with synthetic music or software-engineering results. The following decisions distinguish verified public metadata, author-reported quantities, unresolved content questions and proposed implementation criteria; **no external musical raw dataset was acquired or trained on by this research investigation**.

## Genuine KT candidates expose different unresolved gates

SingPAD's publication reports **1,074 learners, 2,587 note questions, 270 composite knowledge components and 1,080,852 note interactions**. Its cleaning section reports 19,892 recordings, whereas the abstract reports 19,893; that discrepancy must remain visible until raw counts resolve it. Forty-three core pitch, interval and duration concepts form the composite taxonomy. The released schema documents learner, score and recording identifiers, `create_time` completion timestamps, binary `qa_array` note outcomes and `qc_array` question-to-concept mappings. Outcomes derive from automatic assessment, not independent teacher adjudication. Published cleaning removes low-scoring/noisy records, excludes learners with fewer than five records and randomly caps histories at 30 recordings; these choices restrict the population and can alter the chronology retained for modeling. The publication's five-fold description does not establish learner-disjoint folds. ([EDM 2024 publication](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-short-papers.30/index.html), [author repository](https://github.com/itec-hust/singKT-dataset))

OcarinaKT's publisher describes a **two-week challenge with 1,442 participants and 5,242,616 note interactions**. The author-uploaded manuscript additionally reports 97,104 recordings, 15 pieces and 791 questions, and describes learner-disjoint evaluation. However, its **35-KC** statement conflicts with the repository's IDs 0–35: 13 pitch, seven fingering-transition, 11 note-value and five rhythmic-pattern entries, totaling **36**. RecordDS lists learner, piece and record IDs plus pitch/rhythm score arrays; it documents no completion timestamp. Admission to later levels requires passing preceding ones. Therefore record IDs cannot simply become assumed chronological timestamps, score arrays cannot become binary labels through an invented threshold, and results among progressing participants cannot represent all beginning learners. The paper's published AUCs are author measurements, not reproduced DÉCLIC results. ([publisher chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-29755-6_50), [author manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408027676_MPKT_A_Knowledge_Tracing_Model_for_Music_Performance_Learning), [author repository](https://github.com/itec-hust/ocarinaKT-dataset))

Once rights are established, the appropriate SingKT unit is an **entire completed recording**: forecast its notes from prior recordings, then reveal its note outcomes together. Teacher-forcing earlier notes from that same performance answers a different task and must be separately named. Lifetime learner averages, total practice counts and score-wide aggregates are leakage risks; causal features must be recomputed from the visible prefix and training learners only. Singing and ocarina ontologies should remain source-specific. Successful external singing or wind validation would establish a bounded next-performance methods result, not piano transfer, DÉCLIC learner improvement or recommendation effectiveness. These are methodological conclusions from the documented schemas, not claims that a local experiment already ran.

## Licensed piano practice supports a bounded case study

Rach3 is the clearest inspected openly licensed longitudinal piano corpus: its authors report **3,152 MIDI files from four pianists over four years**. Three pianists are advanced and one is a beginner. Filenames encode performer, calendar date, daily session, within-session order and piece identifier; the public beginner directory includes Hanon recordings on consecutive dates. That proves repeated performer/repertoire coverage at file-list level, not successful learning or scored improvement. The catalog distinguishes works with and without an available score, and some files combine sections. **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** supplies an explicit noncommercial research route with attribution/share-alike conditions; it does not establish unrestricted commercial deployment. ([official repository](https://github.com/Rach3Project/rach3_midi_dataset), [license](https://github.com/Rach3Project/rach3_midi_dataset/blob/main/LICENSE), [beginner directory](https://github.com/Rach3Project/rach3_midi_dataset/tree/main/rehearsals/p3), [piece catalog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Rach3Project/rach3_midi_dataset/main/List_of_pieces_with_scores.csv))

A defensible initial acquisition is a **pinned, bounded metadata/MIDI subset**, preserving original filenames, source identities, dates, session order, license and hashes. Alignment against an available reference can derive transparent pitch/rhythm repetition or stability measurements, but those measurements remain provisional unless a teacher-approved rubric validates their meaning. Day/session/order chronology cannot support invented precise between-session time gaps. With four people, a conventional four-way learner partition allocates roughly one pianist to each role and cannot deliver stable population estimates. A within-pianist chronological prefix-to-future-session evaluation is a useful personalized case study; leave-one-pianist-out adds a highly uncertain transfer analysis. Notes, excerpts and takes must not be rebranded as independent learners. No such alignment, label derivation or trained case study was performed by this research note.

PianoVAM is a second genuine practice candidate. Its original paper describes **106 solo recordings from 10 amateurs, approximately 21 hours over six months**, self-labeled DailyPractice; the authors explicitly caution against evaluating them by direct score comparison. The current v1.2 card distinguishes 107 recordings from 106 solo recordings and supplies timestamp filenames, canonical metadata, MIDI-derived note timing/pitch/velocity, automatic fingering labels and an 11-recording manually annotated subset. Fingering labels indicate which finger played, not whether it was pedagogically correct. The current data card states CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, while the older code README states CC BY-NC 4.0: pin the actual data revision and preserve its specific conditions. Raw metadata/TSV URLs did not yield usable content in this investigation; rendered examples verified note fields but did not establish repeated performer-piece coverage. The next decision is **metadata-only inspection and independent repeated-session counting before a bounded raw acquisition**, not immediate KT training. ([original paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08800v1), [current dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PianoVAM/PianoVAM_v1), [code README](https://github.com/yonghyunk1m/PianoVAM-Code))

## Tutoring claims and static corpora require separate roles

A 2026 APDTS publication cites Kaggle's `colabsss/music-learner-performance-dataset` and reports **8,867 learner-session records from 1,245 learners**, with practice duration, pitch/rhythm/tempo/expression measurements and skill levels. These are publisher-reported claims. Neither the original card nor public API attempts returned usable source content here, so collection provenance, licensing, chronology, stable exercises, label construction and repetition remain unverified. The lead cannot justify acquisition or training, and it should not be called synthetic without evidence either. ([publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-026-01988-5), [original Kaggle lead](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/colabsss/music-learner-performance-dataset))

Assessment and transcription resources remain valuable for distinct tasks. Their scale does not turn them into chronological learner histories.

The focused investigation covered GitHub, Zenodo, Hugging Face, Kaggle leads and relevant primary publications. Its negative finding is **no immediately ready and rights-verified KT table among the investigated candidates**, not a universal claim that no musical dataset exists. Static ratings can inform a teacher-facing rubric; transcription corpora can validate another axis's measurements. Neither supplies DÉCLIC mastery ground truth without the missing longitudinal and pedagogical evidence.

Conditional real training begins only after an applicable grant is archived, raw bytes/releases are pinned, chronology and response arrays are audited, stable learner/exercise identifiers and source KCs are preserved, and score conversions/rubrics are documented. Train/selection/calibration/test splits must separate learners where the corpus size permits; chronological case studies must disclose their different generalization target. Entire performances remain atomic, item difficulty uses training learners only, and all current/future outcomes remain hidden at prediction. Report raw and calibrated metrics, multiple seeds/splits, learner-cluster uncertainty, feature-matched statistical and exposure-only controls, and negative findings. Teacher validation and recommendation learning benefits require additional prospective evidence; they do not follow from next-outcome accuracy.

## Conclusion

The existing upgrade's 102 runs established reproducible results on **real software-engineering histories and synthetic musical histories**, not real musical KT. Its stronger posthoc cross-KC statistical control left all three paired intervals spanning zero, so improved primary-study neural results cannot be uniquely attributed to recurrence. This is a useful methodological constraint for the next music study: equalize information visibility before interpreting model sophistication. ([completed upgrade report](C:/Users/moall/Documents/Codex/2026-10-06/files-mentioned-by-the-user-declic/outputs/DECLIC_AXIS7_UPGRADE_REPORT.pdf))

DÉCLIC can now pursue two honest routes in parallel: rights-cleared external singing/wind forecasting for bounded methods validation, and licensed piano-practice case studies plus teacher-grounded collection for the intended product. The learner experience should explain what was observed, which exercise is suggested, why that suggestion follows, and how much evidence supports it. Scientific/source details belong in research and teacher views. Keeping these readiness states separate allows a useful coach and stronger integration today while preserving the credibility of a future real-musical-learning claim.
