# Real piano histories expose forecasting limits

**DÉCLIC now has acquired, explicitly licensed real piano histories, but no usable musical assessment targets and no newly trained real musical forecasting model.** Phase 4 inspected 12 Rach3 MIDI recordings from four pianists and canonical PianoVAM metadata describing 106 solo recordings from ten participants. The derived case study identifies 20 repeated learner–piece groups, including 15 spanning multiple calendar days. Those observations establish genuine repeated practice, not correct performances, improvement or mastery. SingKT remains the strongest documented annotated next-performance candidate, but its applicable raw-data grant was not established; OcarinaKT also lacks that grant and verified chronological ordering. The correct Phase 4 result is a reproducible descriptive piano case study alongside a conditional forecasting protocol, with missing assessment evidence kept explicit. Findings below distinguish source publications, acquired bytes, local calculations and future evaluation proposals; verification date is **13 October 2026**.

## Chronology supports future-day partitions without learning labels

Rach3 filenames encode performer, calendar date, daily sitting and within-sitting piece order. No precise wall-clock event time is available. A documented filename inconsistency matters: its 25-character rehearsal stems leave a 14-character piece suffix after the 11-character chronology prefix, although the README describes a 12-character piece identifier. Preserve the complete opaque suffix instead of truncating it. PianoVAM filenames provide local date and time but no established timezone; inventing UTC offsets would create false precision. Exact composer/piece strings are conservative grouping keys and can hide movement or naming differences. ([Rach3 filename specification](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Rach3Project/rach3_midi_dataset/a9519492b086a6c039a9d0994689ca0f279186bd/README.md), [PianoVAM metadata](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PianoVAM/PianoVAM_v1/resolve/1f039ab9d6128626bff708faad6472d220b50eaa/metadata.json))

The derived artifact separates earlier observations from future whole calendar days within learner–piece histories. It explicitly records **target unavailable**, **not learner-disjoint**, **zero usable assessment targets**, **zero trained models**, and **real musical predictive validity not established**. Recording duration and note count describe activity; they cannot become correctness, fluency or improvement labels through an invented threshold. PianoVAM's static participant skill bands likewise cannot serve as repeated performance outcomes. Its manual fingering annotations identify actual hand/finger choices for 1,800 notes in 11 recordings; they do not adjudicate pedagogically good fingering. Producing transcription, alignment or technique classifiers belongs to upstream axes and was not performed here. ([Actual case-study results](C:/Users/moall/Documents/Codex/2026-10-06/files-mentioned-by-the-user-declic/outputs/declic-axis7/artifacts/phase4/music-case-study.json), [annotation specification](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PianoVAM/PianoVAM_v1/raw/1f039ab9d6128626bff708faad6472d220b50eaa/README.md))

Four Rach3 people cannot support stable population-level neural validation. Neither notes nor repeated takes create additional independent learners. PianoVAM's source transcription train/test tags are also not a learner-disjoint KT evaluation design. A descriptive calendar demonstrates that real chronological histories are accessible; it makes no claim that a model predicts their future learning.

## Annotated singing and wind candidates retain unresolved gates

Current full repository trees and READMEs still establish **no applicable explicit raw-data reuse grant** for SingKT or OcarinaKT. Their pinned commits remain `7f64d4785535761820a165087570cd1bb86274fa` and `1681647ba428517283b2ddea616cb73b7fe4c67c`; both repository APIs return `license:null`. This conclusion combines source inspection with publication/deposit evidence, rather than relying on the API alone. Public visibility, a paper's license and third-party reuse do not satisfy this project's explicit dataset-grant gate. No SingKT/Ocarina raw tables were acquired or trained on, and no author was contacted. This is a scoped readiness decision, not a universal judgment about every possible research use. ([SingKT source](https://github.com/itec-hust/singKT-dataset/blob/7f64d4785535761820a165087570cd1bb86274fa/README.md), [OcarinaKT source](https://github.com/itec-hust/ocarinaKT-dataset/blob/1681647ba428517283b2ddea616cb73b7fe4c67c/README.md), [paper-only Zenodo deposit](https://zenodo.org/records/12729828))

SingKT documents learner/recording identifiers, completion timestamps, native binary note outcomes and note-to-concept arrays. SingPAD reports **1,074 learners and 1,080,852 cleaned note interactions**, while conflicting 19,892/19,893 recording counts remain unresolved locally. Automatic assessment generated those outcomes; they are not independently teacher-validated mastery. Cleaning excludes some weak/noisy records and short histories and randomly caps histories, which limits generalization. OcarinaKT documents pitch/rhythm scores but no timestamp field, and passing earlier levels controls later participation. Its 36 listed concept IDs also conflict with the earlier paper's 35-KC description. Record IDs cannot be assumed chronological, and unspecified score scales cannot be converted into binary successes. ([SingPAD original paper](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-short-papers.30/2024.EDM-short-papers.30.pdf), [OcarinaKT schema](https://github.com/itec-hust/ocarinaKT-dataset/blob/1681647ba428517283b2ddea616cb73b7fe4c67c/README.md))

## Next-performance validation must hide the whole performance

Once rights, chronology and target semantics pass, a valid next-performance experiment predicts an entire upcoming recording from **strictly earlier completed recordings**. Current-recording outcomes remain hidden until every prediction is scored, then enter history atomically. Predicting later notes after observing earlier notes of that same take answers a separate online task. Singing results would establish prediction of that source assessment engine, not transfer to piano or recommendation effectiveness. ([SingKT recording-level schema](https://github.com/itec-hust/singKT-dataset/blob/7f64d4785535761820a165087570cd1bb86274fa/README.md))

The proposed evaluation should separate training, selection, calibration and test learners where sample size permits; personalized future-day case studies must state their different generalization scope. Recompute lifetime averages and exposure from visible prefixes, fit difficulty and calibration on permitted subsets, and compare recurrence against matched-information statistical, exposure-only and recent-outcome controls. Current/future-label perturbation checks should leave earlier predictions unchanged. Report log loss, Brier score, defined AUC, calibration, cold-start/unseen-item slices and learner-cluster uncertainty. These are proposed safeguards, **not experiments already executed on the acquired piano histories**.

## Conclusion

Phase 4 replaces an unresolved piano coverage question with inspectable real histories while exposing the remaining dependency precisely: meaningful, versioned assessment outcomes. Keeping descriptive practice evidence separate from prediction eligibility allows useful data integration today and preserves the credibility of a later musical forecasting claim. The next decisive scientific advance is a rights-cleared annotated source or teacher-grounded longitudinal piano judgments, rather than relabeling available MIDI activity as mastery.
